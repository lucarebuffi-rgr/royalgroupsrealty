How to Sell a Hoarder House Fast in Texas or Georgia

By Luca Rebuffi – Royal Groups Realty  ·  June 2025  ·  8 min read

If you're reading this, you're probably dealing with a situation most people don't talk about openly. Maybe a parent passed away and left behind a home packed floor-to-ceiling with decades of belongings. Maybe a sibling has been struggling, and the family is finally stepping in. Maybe it's your own home, and you've reached the point where you know something has to change but you don't know where to start.

Whatever the story is — we've seen it before. We've bought hoarder houses across Texas and Georgia for the last six years, and we want to be upfront with you: we don't care what's inside. We buy it as-is. No cleanup, no judgment, no questions. This guide walks through exactly how it works, what your options are, and what to expect.

What Counts as a "Hoarder House"?

The term covers a wide range of situations. Some homes have so much accumulated stuff that hallways are narrow paths and rooms can't be entered. Some have years of unopened mail, newspapers, or items still in their packaging. Some look mostly normal from the outside but have severe interior damage from years of clutter — pet stains, mold, pest infestations, structural issues hidden under piles.

The technical clinical term is Level 1 through Level 5 hoarding on the Clutter Hoarding Scale, where Level 5 involves severe structural damage, sanitation issues, and often unsafe living conditions. But you don't need to label it. If the house has more stuff in it than you can reasonably deal with, this guide is for you.

💛 One thing first — there's no shame here. Hoarding is recognized as a mental health condition by the American Psychiatric Association. If it's a loved one, they're not "lazy" or "gross" — they're dealing with something real. Our job is to help you move forward with the property. That's it.

Why Selling a Hoarder House Through Traditional Channels Is So Hard

Most homeowners try to do the "right thing" first — clean it out, fix it up, list it on the MLS. Here's why that path usually breaks down:

1. The cleanout alone can cost $5,000–$30,000+

Professional hoarder house cleanouts in Texas and Georgia typically run anywhere from $5,000 for a Level 2 to $30,000+ for a severe Level 4 or 5. That's before any repairs. Add hazmat-rated companies for biohazard cleanup and the cost climbs further.

2. The repairs after cleanout are often worse

Once the stuff is gone, you typically find what's underneath — damaged flooring, ruined drywall, pet damage, mold, pest infestations, broken plumbing, and sometimes structural problems. Many homes need a full gut renovation: $50K–$150K depending on size and condition.

3. Most retail buyers won't touch it

Even after cleanout, the home's history makes financing difficult. FHA and VA loans require the home to meet condition standards. Most retail buyers can't get a mortgage on a property in poor condition. You're left with cash investors anyway — but now you've spent tens of thousands prepping for them.

4. The emotional weight is real

Sorting through a parent's lifetime of belongings is one of the hardest things a family can do. Spending months doing it while grieving — and arguing with siblings about what to keep — is exhausting. Many families burn out and the house sits for years.

How We Buy Hoarder Houses — The Honest Version

We're investors. We buy hoarder homes specifically because most other buyers won't. Our process is built around making it as low-friction as possible for you:

  1. Reach out — call or text 469-665-8481, or send a quick email to info@royalgroupsrealty.com. Tell us where the property is and roughly what condition it's in. You don't need to send photos if you don't want to.
  2. Quick walkthrough or virtual look — for hoarder homes, we usually do a brief in-person walkthrough (or a video walkthrough if you're out of state). We're not there to judge. We just need to see what's there so we can make a fair offer.
  3. Cash offer in 24 hours — based on the home's location, lot value, comparable sales, and what it'll cost us to clean it out and renovate.
  4. Take what you want, leave the rest — this is the part most sellers can't believe. Walk through the house, take any items that matter to you — family photos, keepsakes, documents, anything sentimental. Leave everything else exactly where it is. We handle the entire cleanout at no cost to you.
  5. Close in 7–21 days at a licensed title company in your state. Funds wired or check at closing.

📦 Important: Before you sell, walk through and pull out anything sentimental — photo albums, jewelry, important documents (deeds, wills, tax records), family heirlooms, military awards, anything irreplaceable. Once we close, the cleanout starts and items don't come back. Take your time on this step — we'll work around your timeline.

Hoarder House Sale vs. Cleanout-Then-Sell — Real Numbers

Here's a realistic side-by-side for a moderately severe hoarder home worth around $250,000 in clean, renovated condition. The home itself is structurally sound but needs major cleanout and interior renovation:

Line Item Clean & Renovate, Then List Sell As-Is to Cash Buyer
Sale price $250,000 $135,000
Hoarder cleanout –$15,000 $0
Renovation (flooring, paint, repairs) –$60,000 $0
Agent commission (6%) –$15,000 $0
Closing costs (~2%) –$5,000 $0
6 months of holding costs –$8,000 $0
Net to seller $147,000 $135,000
Timeline ~7 months 7–21 days
Out-of-pocket cost $75,000+ upfront $0

The clean-and-list path appears to net more on paper — but only $12K, after 7 months of work and $75K of upfront cash you have to come up with first. For most sellers dealing with a hoarder house, the cash sale isn't just easier — it's the only realistic option when you don't have $75K sitting around to invest in renovation.

Common Hoarder House Situations We Handle

Inherited Hoarder Home

This is the most common scenario we see. A parent or relative passed away and left behind a lifetime of belongings. The family lives out of state, doesn't have time to fly back and forth, and is overwhelmed by the scope. We've closed dozens of these. Read our related guide: Selling an Inherited Home in Texas.

Living Hoarder Selling Their Own Home

Sometimes the homeowner themselves reaches out — often after pressure from family, a near-miss with code enforcement, or a health scare. This takes real courage, and we treat it with the respect it deserves. No lectures, no judgment.

Family Stepping In

Adult children helping an aging parent transition to assisted living. The home needs to be sold to fund care. The contents are a lifetime of accumulation. We work directly with you, the parent, and any care coordinators or attorneys involved.

Code Enforcement or Neighbor Complaints

If the city has gotten involved — citations, fines, condemnation warnings — time matters. We can close fast enough to resolve most code situations before they escalate. Read more: Selling a House With Code Violations.

Estate or Probate Hoarder Property

We work with estate attorneys and executors regularly. We can sign a purchase agreement while probate is open and close once authority is granted. See: Probate Real Estate in Texas & Georgia.

What If the House Has Biohazard Issues?

Some hoarder homes go beyond clutter — animal waste, deceased animals, biohazard contamination, severe mold, structural damage from years of moisture. We still buy these. We've handled homes that hazmat companies need to clear before our renovation crew can even walk in. You don't have to do any of that. We absorb the full cost of remediation in our offer.

What If the Property Has Pests?

Rodents, roaches, fleas, bedbugs — common in hoarder homes and not a deal-breaker for us. We handle extermination as part of cleanout. Don't let pest concerns stop you from reaching out.

How to Get Started — Without Feeling Overwhelmed

The hardest part for most sellers is making the first call. Here's how to make it easier on yourself:

  • Don't try to clean anything first. Seriously. Don't waste a Saturday hauling bags out before calling. The condition you're looking at right now is the condition we expect.
  • You don't need photos. If you'd rather not photograph the inside, you don't have to. We can do a discreet walkthrough whenever works for you.
  • You don't have to clear the place out before our visit. Just open the door.
  • Bring whoever you trust. A sibling, a friend, an attorney, a real estate agent. We talk with whoever you want involved.
  • Take your time deciding. Our offer is good for at least a week. You don't have to commit on the spot.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will you really buy it as-is — no cleanup at all?

Yes. We've bought homes where stuff was piled to the ceiling. Homes with pets still living inside. Homes that hadn't been entered in years. As-is means as-is.

How do you determine the offer?

We calculate what the home would sell for after a full cleanout and renovation, then subtract our estimated cleanout cost, renovation cost, holding costs, agent fees we'd pay to resell, and our margin. We share the breakdown openly — you'll see exactly how we got to our number.

What if I'm embarrassed about the condition?

We've walked through homes that would make most people speechless. We don't react. We don't take photos to share with anyone. We don't post anything online. This is a business transaction handled with discretion.

Do you work with hoarders directly, or only with family?

Both. We've worked directly with hoarders who reached out themselves. We've also worked with families intervening on behalf of a relative. Either way, we treat everyone with respect.

What if I want to keep some items but can't sort through everything?

We can give you time to do a slow walk-through and pull out anything that matters. We've worked with sellers who took two or three visits to comfortable sort through what they wanted. We close on your timeline.

What about important documents — wills, deeds, photos, money hidden in the house?

This is exactly why we encourage sellers to do a thorough walk-through before closing. People often hide cash, jewelry, savings bonds, and important documents in hoarder homes. Take your time. Once we close, items belong to us and get disposed of during cleanout.

Will the neighbors find out?

Not from us. We don't put signs in the yard. We don't advertise the address. The sale never goes on the MLS. As far as neighbors are concerned, the property quietly changed hands.

Do you buy hoarder houses with mortgages still on them?

Yes. The mortgage is paid off at closing from the sale proceeds. Whatever's left after the payoff goes to you.

What areas do you serve?

All of Texas and all of Georgia. Major metros and rural counties alike. From Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, Austin, and San Antonio in Texas to Atlanta, Marietta, Savannah, Augusta, and Macon in Georgia.
