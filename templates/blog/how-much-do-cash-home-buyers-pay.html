How Much Do Cash Home Buyers Actually Pay?

By Luca Rebuffi – Royal Groups Realty  ·  August 2026  ·  7 min read
The Reality

There's No Single Number — But There Is a Formula

It's the first question almost every homeowner asks before picking up the phone: "How much will you actually pay for my house?" It's a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends — on condition, location, comparable sales, and how much work the property needs. But "it depends" isn't a satisfying answer, so this guide breaks down exactly how cash offers are calculated, what percentage of market value to expect, and how the real numbers compare to a traditional listing once every cost is accounted for.

The Short Answer

Expect 70–85% of Fair Market Value

Most reputable cash buyers, us included, offer somewhere between 70% and 85% of a home's fair market value. Where a property lands in that range depends mostly on condition: a home that's move-in ready with only cosmetic issues will land toward the higher end, while a property needing a new roof, foundation work, or a full interior gut will land closer to 70%.

That percentage covers the repair costs we'll take on, the holding costs and resale risk we're absorbing, and our margin for buying without an inspection contingency or financing fallout. It's not an arbitrary discount — it's the cost of certainty.

💡 A cash offer isn't measured against your home's top-dollar retail price alone. It should be measured against what you'd actually walk away with after commissions, repairs, and months of carrying costs on a traditional sale.

The Formula

How We Actually Calculate an Offer

There's no black box here. Every offer we make follows the same basic math:

  1. After Repair Value (ARV): What the home would sell for on the open market once it's fully repaired and updated, based on recent comparable sales in the immediate area — not a Zillow estimate.
  2. Subtract estimated repair costs: Roof, HVAC, foundation, cosmetic updates — whatever it would take to bring the property to market-ready condition.
  3. Subtract holding and selling costs: Property taxes, insurance, utilities, and closing costs we'll carry until the home resells.
  4. Subtract our minimum margin: The smallest amount we need to make the deal work as a business, not the maximum we could get away with.

What's left after those four steps is the cash offer you receive — usually within 24 hours of us seeing the property or a few clear photos and a condition rundown.

Factors that move the number up

Good bones, a functional roof and HVAC, no foundation issues, a desirable neighborhood, and clean title all push an offer toward the higher end of the range.

Factors that move the number down

Structural repairs, code violations, extensive deferred maintenance, title complications, or a property that's sat vacant and deteriorating all increase our risk — and lower the number.
