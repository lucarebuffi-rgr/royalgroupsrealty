How to Avoid Foreclosure in Georgia

By Luca Rebuffi – Royal Groups Realty  ·  October 2026  ·  6 min read
The Reality

Georgia Foreclosures Move Faster Than Most States

Georgia uses a non-judicial foreclosure process, meaning a lender doesn't need to go through court to foreclose. That makes Georgia's timeline significantly faster than judicial-foreclosure states, and it means homeowners often have less time to act than they realize. If you've received a notice of default or missed several payments, understanding the timeline and your options matters more here than almost anywhere else.

How It Works

Georgia's Non-Judicial Process

After a default, the lender typically sends required notices, then advertises the sale in the county's legal organ (a designated newspaper) once a week for four consecutive weeks. The foreclosure sale itself takes place on the courthouse steps on the first Tuesday of the month. There's no court hearing required in most cases, which is why the process can move faster than in states requiring judicial foreclosure.

💡 Because there's no mandatory court process, the window to act in Georgia can be shorter than homeowners expect. Reaching out as soon as you're behind — not after a notice arrives — gives you the most options.

What to Do

Steps to Take If You're Facing Foreclosure

  1. Contact your lender immediately. Ask about forbearance, repayment plans, or loan modification options before exploring anything else.
  2. Understand your specific notice timeline. Confirm exactly when the sale is scheduled so you know how much time you have.
  3. Explore loss mitigation options. Forbearance, modification, or a formal short sale through your lender may be available.
  4. Consider a cash sale before the sale date. Selling before the courthouse sale lets you control the outcome and protect any remaining equity.
  5. Close before the sale date. A completed sale stops the foreclosure process and can protect your credit compared to letting the sale proceed.
