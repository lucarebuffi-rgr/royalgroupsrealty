Dallas Market Context

The Dallas Real Estate Market in 2026

Dallas County’s real estate market has cooled from its 2021–2022 peak but remains one of the most active in the country. The median home price in Dallas has stabilized around $280,000–$320,000 depending on neighborhood, with days-on-market stretching to 45–75 days for properties that need work.

Here’s the problem that most sellers don’t see until they’re in it: Dallas has a significant two-tier market. Move-in-ready homes in East Dallas, Lake Highlands, and North Dallas still move quickly with multiple offers. But distressed homes — properties with foundation issues from the clay soil, outdated systems, deferred maintenance, or title complications — sit. Buyers want turnkey. If your home isn’t turnkey, the traditional listing process becomes expensive and slow.

Property taxes in Dallas County run roughly 2.2–2.5% of assessed value per year. On a $300,000 home, that’s $6,600–$7,500 per year, or $550–$625 per month just in taxes, accumulating every day the home doesn’t sell.

Option 2: List with a Real Estate Agent

The traditional route — listing with a Realtor — can work well in a hot market, but it typically takes 45–90 days to close and comes with 5–6% in agent commissions, plus repair costs and closing fees. In a slower market or with a distressed property, this timeline can stretch even longer. Buyers making offers contingent on financing fall through at roughly 15–20% of the time, meaning you may start the process over after months of waiting.

Option 3: For Sale By Owner (FSBO)

Selling on your own cuts out agent fees but requires significant time, marketing knowledge, and negotiation experience. Most FSBO sellers in Dallas end up taking longer to sell and accepting lower offers than agent-listed homes — and they still pay 2–3% to the buyer’s agent.

A Dallas Specialty

Foundation Issues in Dallas: The Biggest Obstacle to a Fast Sale

Dallas County sits on Blackland Prairie — one of the most expansive clay soil formations in North America. When it rains, the clay swells. During the summer droughts that hit Dallas annually, it shrinks. This cycle of expansion and contraction causes foundation movement in an estimated 60%+ of Dallas-area homes.

Foundation issues are the #1 reason Dallas home sales fall through. A retail buyer gets an inspection, the inspector flags foundation movement, the buyer either walks or demands a repair credit of $8,000–$40,000+. The deal collapses or the seller takes a hit they weren’t expecting.

We buy Dallas homes with foundation issues as-is. Pier and beam foundations, slab foundations, partial repairs, no repairs — we’ve seen it all. The foundation problem is factored into our offer, and we close without any surprise renegotiations after inspection. Read our full guide: Selling a House With Foundation Issues in Texas.
