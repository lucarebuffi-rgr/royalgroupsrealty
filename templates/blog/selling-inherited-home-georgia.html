Selling an Inherited Home in Georgia

By Luca Rebuffi – Royal Groups Realty  ·  May 2025  ·  6 min read

Inheriting a home in Georgia can be both a blessing and an overwhelming responsibility. Between probate court, property taxes, maintenance, and family dynamics, many heirs find that selling quickly is the most practical path forward. This guide walks you through everything you need to know.

Does the Home Need to Go Through Probate in Georgia?

In most cases, yes. Georgia requires that inherited real estate go through the probate process before it can be sold — unless the property was held in a trust or had a joint tenancy with right of survivorship. Probate in Georgia typically takes 6–12 months, but the property can often be marketed and sold during that process with court approval.

Common Challenges with Inherited Homes

  • Multiple heirs who disagree on what to do with the property
  • Property hasn't been maintained and needs significant repairs
  • Outstanding mortgage, property taxes, or liens
  • Heirs live out of state and can't manage the property
  • Emotional difficulty making decisions about a family home

Selling an Inherited Home As-Is

One of the biggest advantages of working with a cash buyer is that you don't need to repair, clean, or update the inherited property. We buy homes in any condition across all 35 Georgia counties we serve — including Cobb County, DeKalb County, Henry County, and Cherokee County.

What About Capital Gains Tax?

Inherited properties receive a "stepped-up basis" — meaning your cost basis is the fair market value at the time of inheritance, not the original purchase price. This significantly reduces capital gains tax if you sell quickly after inheriting. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

If There Are Multiple Heirs

All heirs must agree to sell. If one heir refuses, it may require a partition action through the courts. However, in most cases, heirs ultimately agree that a quick cash sale is the most practical solution — especially when the property needs repairs or is generating ongoing costs.
